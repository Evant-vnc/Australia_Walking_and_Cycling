The trails

Five kinds of trail, built for different trips

Walking tracks are long and rough. Rail trails are short and gentle. Bikes get their own cycling trails and mountain-bike parks.

Only rail trails welcome everyone

Main useAllowedOn some trailsNot intended

Each trail type is built for a main user base. Walking, cycling and Mountain-bike trail are made specified for specific purposes. Rail trails are the exception, all allow walkers, most allow bikes and only Rail trail allows wheelchair access.

A rail trail takes an afternoon, a walking track several days

Most rail trails are under 50 km. The 5,330 km Bicentennial Trail runs off the edge.

The people

Women walk more, men ride more

Half of all adults walk for fun. Bushwalking peaks in middle age, then halves after 65.

The gender gap flips between walking and riding

MenWomenLine colour = activity

Women lead in walking for fun and bush walking. Men held the gap for cycling. And the ratio for Mountain biking is about four parts men to one part women. So walking trail serves a wider public and biking trails serves a narrow mostly male group.

Where the listed trails are

Rail trails crowd the south-east along old railway lines near old cities. Long tracks run through the remote north, west and south, further from populated areas.

Trail kilometres for every 100,000 people

Walking tracksRail + cycling trails

NT, SA abd Tasmania has the most tracks. NSW the most populous state has only about 1km of rail and cycling trail per 10000 people against national average of 15km. Trail does not follow population areas.

Supply meets demand

More trail does not mean more walkers

South Australia has plenty of track per person but the fewest bushwalkers. Canberra rides most with no listed rail trails.

Access

Flat trails matter for older walkers

Walkers over 65 need gentle paths. Only 39 of 115 rail trails list wheelchair access, and none in WA.

About one in three rail trails is listed as suitable for a wheelchair.

Wheelchair-friendly rail trails, by state

Share suitableLabel = suitable / listed

Access depends on the state. The NT and SA lead, WA lists none of its 17 trails. A wheelchair user's choices depend more on which state they visit than on how many trail it has.

Change

The trails keep growing, the crowds hold steady

New trails open every few years. Participation barely moved between 2024 and 2025.

Participation barely moved, 2024 to 2025

Bar colour = activityRight = rose · left = fellGrey text = 2024 → 2025 share

Bushwalking rose 0.6 points and cycling fell 0.3. Every change is under a point, but is expected in survey error. Habits seems to hold steady from 2024 to 2025.

Conclusion

Trails and people do not always line up

Australia keeps adding trails. Who uses them depends on who lives nearby, and how old they are.
